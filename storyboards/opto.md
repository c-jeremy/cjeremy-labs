# Pip讲光遗传学: storyboard (177.5 s, 96 bpm, Mandarin VO)

Audience: middle/high-school students with some biology, so show the mechanisms, not just the story.

Logline: Pip is lost in the brain's tangle, where nobody can tell which cell does what. Pip meets a light-seeking pond
alga (Chlamy), learns how its light-gated channel works (retinal isomerisation, cation influx, threshold, action
potential), how it is put into one chosen kind of neuron (virus + promoter), and switched off again (chloride pump),
and comes back to the tangle with Chlamy as its lamp (2026 Nobel: Hegemann, Nagel, Deisseroth).

World / colour arc: night-indigo brain → sunlit pond green → pale lab teal (egg, membrane) → dark violet (neuron
membrane, dish) → indigo tissue (virus) → warm sand (mouse) → dark violet (off switch) → blind dark warming to amber
(clinic) → the opening indigo tangle, now lit.

Continuity:
- Chlamy is Pip's companion from the pond on: it swims beside Pip in every later shot and is the lamp at the end.
- One membrane carries the micro scenes: the egg membrane match-cuts (cream flash) to the neuron membrane; the action
  potential runs off to the right and lands as a bead on the dish neuron's axon.
- One voltage trace (cream panel, dashed threshold) in D, E and H.
- Our eye's rhodopsin (B, the slow cascade) comes back in C beside channelrhodopsin: the same retinal.
- The camera dives into the eyespot (B → C) and into the mouse's brain window (G → H).
- Signals always run left → right. The ending rhymes with the opening (same tangle; now one circuit lights).

Colour code: ChR2 = rose, retinal = gold ring + zigzag, blue light = pale cornflower, Na+ = red disc with cream +,
resting inside charge = blue disc with −, Cl− = green disc with −, NpHR pump = amber, voltage-gated Na channel = teal,
promoter = gold block at the gene's head, AAV = pale hexagon, the gene = green beads.
